*Written Oct 20, 2026 for Lane. **Revision 2 (Oct 20, after an adversarial review):** two independent no-context
reviewers attacked revision 1, one against the code and the data sources, one for anything that could mislead a voter
or treat a side unevenly. Their 44 findings (A's 22 plus the four parts of A23; B's 18) were argued down in
"Adversarial review (Oct 20)" at the end: 43 survived (two of them changed) and are folded in below; one was rejected,
along with parts of three others, and the struck-through ones stay there with the reason they died. Status:
**draft for Lane's review.** Nothing has been dry-run on a copy of the repo yet: Task 1 (rehearsals on real,

## The request (verbatim)

> "is there an automated way to pull information about who won each election in Travis County on November 4 and
> later? If so, I'd like to explore how we might show people a version of their ballot that tells who won, and whether
> or not it matched their pick, when they come back after the election has ended."

> unofficial and make that clear as we get the detail — again, the next day is fine, it doesn't need to be immediate,

> something has been made official."

> assumption in mind."

## Decisions already made (Lane, Oct 20)

1. **Next day, not election night.** Nothing changes on the night of Nov 3. The first results appear the morning of
   Nov 4.
2. **Unofficial first, clearly labeled; then official, contest by contest.** Every result carries "Unofficial" or
   "Official", the time its numbers were posted, and where they came from.
3. **Too close to call:** a margin under 1 point names no leader. **May go to a runoff:** in a contest that needs a
   majority, a top candidate between 48% and 52%. *(Revision 2 adds guards to both: decision 2 below, and the rules.)*
4. **Updates are shown in the page** ("Since you last looked: …"). No push, no email.
5. **`results.json` is exempt from the VERSION bump rule** (its own sequence and its own check), but every change to
   the *wording* of a verdict goes through a pull request Lane merges.
6. **County files on Clarity are downloaded by hand** (Clarity refuses cloud servers). The Texas SOS feed is fetched
   by a script. If the counties say a script is welcome (Lane's notes, Oct 20), Task 9 automates it.

## Decisions for Lane (before Task 2)

1. **Where results show (recommended: three places).** A ribbon on each contest (both layouts), a Result column in
   At a glance, and one line at the top of the ballot ("Results: 14 of 31 official · oldest numbers from Nov 6,
   9:10 am") plus "Since you last looked" when something changed. The crib sheet and landing page don't change.
   Results show for contests without a pick too, with no "your pick" line.
2. **Guards on "leading" (recommended: yes).** The 1-point rule alone can name a leader on a single vote in a small
   contest (MUD confirmation districts are tiny), or while thousands of late mail, overseas, military and provisional
   ballots are still uncounted (they keep arriving until Nov 9; Travis's 2025 count grew 218 votes after the state
   feed stopped). Proposed: a leader is named only when the margin is ≥ 1 point **and** larger, in votes, than every
   ballot that could still change it (the outstanding-ballot bound in the rules); contests with fewer than 1,000 votes
   show counts only, with no leader, until official; state races also need every county reporting.
3. **"Won" before the canvass? (recommended: no).** You asked "who won". Under these rules the word "Won" appears only
   when a contest is official: Nov 6–17 for local and county races, Nov 21–Dec 7 for most state and federal races,
   and **January 2027 for Governor and Lieutenant Governor** (the Legislature canvasses those two). Until then the
   ribbon says "Leading", or, once the county's last late ballots are counted (about Nov 10), "Leading in the final
   unofficial count". An "Apparent winner" label is the alternative; it reads like a call, which Ballotta doesn't make.
4. **Friend links (recommended: later).** "You both picked…" touches the share format; phase 2.
5. **One anonymous count (recommended: `results_seen`, no details, once per visit)**, through the privacy firewall and
   into the README's list. Or none.
6. **Hays by script (recommended: yes).** Hays's PDFs are on a server with no robots.txt that answers cloud requests;
   a script fetch with a "Ballotta (ballotta.org)" User-Agent saves you one download a day. ACC's two small counties
   (Caldwell, Lee) the same way if their files allow it (Task 1 finds out).
7. **Release day (recommended: Nov 3, after the polls close at 7 pm).** The release that adds the results code shows
   "A newer Ballotta is available" to pages left open. After 7 pm on Election Day nobody needs the crib sheet any
   more, and nothing visible changes until Nov 4. The other option is a quiet evening Oct 29–31 (busiest crib-sheet
   days). Either way, only the page code needs the release; parsers, map and workflow can keep changing until Nov 3.
8. **Two GitHub settings (recommended: yes).** Turn on "Allow GitHub Actions to create … pull requests", and add a
   ruleset on `main` that requires pull requests for `results.json` and `source/results/` (today any write token could
   push to `main`).
9. **When you can't merge.** Nothing goes live without your merge. Either accept that county and state numbers wait
   a day (still labeled with their time), or name a backup person with merge rights for Nov 4–Dec 10.
10. **The words** (Task 5's full table). Visitor-facing words go to you before they ship, as always.

## What "done" looks like

1. From the morning of Nov 4 (Austin time), a voter who finds their ballot sees each contest's result, contest by
   contest: unofficial counts (with a leader only when the rules allow), then **Official** once the body that
   canvasses that contest has canvassed it, with each source's posted time.
2. **Picks are compared on the device.** `results.json` is the same file for everyone; no pick, precinct or address is
   sent. The request for it is a plain GET of a public file from ballotta.org.
3. **Every verdict change has been seen by Lane:** each arrives in a pull request whose description lists, in words,
   what changes, and merges happen at a fixed time each day whatever the outcome.
4. **No verdict from part of an electorate:** every contest's electorate (counties, and for annexations the district
   or the territory) is cited from its election order; a race-wide verdict missing a piece is refused except under the
   partial-data bound.
5. **Even-handed:** every choice looks the same (no party colors, no incumbent marks from the feeds), in ballot order;
   declared write-in candidates appear by name like everyone else.
6. **Tested** against real past elections whose official results are known (Task 1), plus pipeline and browser tests
   and mutants. **No fixture results ever appear on ballotta.org.**

## How it's built

### The files

- `source/results/inbox/<county>/` — where Lane's downloads land, one folder per county (every Clarity download is
  named `detailxml.zip`, so one folder each), each holding a `.keep` so the folder survives being emptied. The
  workflow identifies a file's county from its contents, and refuses a file in the wrong folder.
- `source/results/raw/<source>/<posted-time>/…` — every file, exactly as fetched or downloaded, named by **the time
  posted inside the file** (Detail XML's timestamp; the PDF's Run Date and Run Time; SOS's `LastUpdatedTime`, read as
  Austin time), never the download time. Committed only when its hash is new (so the repo grows by changes, not by
  fetches; SOS `countyInfo` is not fetched at all). Never edited.
- `source/results/excluded.json` — raw files to ignore, each with a reason and a date: how a bad download is
  corrected without editing or deleting history.
- `source/results_map.json` — one entry per contest in the answer key (214; the two "declared elected unopposed"
  groups aren't contests and need none):
  - `source`: `sos` or `counties` (where the numbers come from)
  - `canvasser`: the body whose canvass makes it official (`state`, `legislature`, `travis-county`, `city-of-austin`,
    `austin-isd`, `tcad`, `eastwood-mud`, …)
  - `electorate`: counties (or district / territory), each with a citation (election order URL and page)
  - `keys`: each source's name or ID for the contest
  - `choices`: each choice → each source's spelling, **keyed by the names the page stores picks under**
    (`optionsOf()`: candidate names without the party suffix; FOR / AGAINST), plus every **declared write-in
    candidate** in the source, listed by name and marked write-in
  - `seats` (from the title, as the page's `seats()` reads it), `majority` (sourced: city charters, ISD and college
    rules, TCAD; state, federal and county general-election offices are plurality, §2.001)
  - `depends_on`: for contests whose outcome needs another (MUD and MMD confirmation propositions; ESD annexations
    voted separately by the district and the territory)
- `source/results/official.json` — written by hand: one line per canvassing body, `{canvasser, canvassed: date,
  link}`, plus optional `recount: {requested: date, link}` and `withdrawn` (see Kill switch). A contest is Official only
  when **its own canvasser's** line is here and the official numbers for it are loaded (Official Canvass Report for
  state contests; each county's final file for the rest). The check refuses a canvass date outside the legal window
  (local: Nov 6–17; state: Nov 21–Dec 7; legislature: after Jan 12, 2027).
- `source/build_results.py` — reads all of the above and writes **`results.json`** deterministically (same inputs, same
  bytes: no clock reads), plus the plain-words change list for the pull request.
- `source/check_results.py` — refuses:
  - an answer-key contest missing from the map;
  - **in a contest Ballotta shows**, a source choice that maps to nothing (contests not on Travis ballots are ignored by
    the source's own county or district fields, not listed by hand);
  - a counties contest with a county the map doesn't cite;
  - Official through anyone but the contest's canvasser, or a canvass date outside its window;
  - a source's posted time going backwards (corrections go through `excluded.json`);
  - a `seq` lower than the live one (the `results.json` at `origin/main`);
  - numbers that don't add up (choices vs. totals; county sums vs. an entity's canvass; Travis's slice vs. SOS's
    race-wide only as a warning, since SOS's county figures freeze);
  - a committed `results.json` that differs from a fresh build (as `build_site.py --check` does for the page).

### `results.json`

```
{ "seq": 7, "election": "2026-11-03", "withdrawn": false,
  "contests": { "c071": {
      "state": "counting" | "unofficial" | "partial" | "final_unofficial" | "official" | "recount",
      "verdict": "none" | "leading" | "too_close" | "may_runoff" | "runoff" | "elected" | "won" | "passing" | "failing"
                 | "passed" | "failed" | "unopposed" | "tied_recount" | "tied_runoff" | "failed_tie",
      "leaders": ["FOR"], "runoff": [],
      "choices": [["FOR", 123456, 61.2], ["AGAINST", 78345, 38.8]],   // ballot order; write-ins by name at the end
      "missing": ["Hays"], "travis": [["FOR", 100345, 62.0], ["AGAINST", 61500, 38.0]],
      "posted": { "travis": "2026-11-04T08:41", "williamson": "2026-11-04T07:55", "hays": null },
      "changed": 6, "corrected": false,
      "note": "district_not_confirmed" | null } } }
```

- `seq` goes up by one with every merge (taken from the inputs: the count of merged result updates), never a clock.
- `changed` is the `seq` at which this contest's state or verdict last changed; "Since you last looked" counts
  contests with `changed` above the `seq` the device last showed. `corrected` marks a verdict that went backwards
  (a correction), shown as "Corrected since you last looked" for a few days.
- A placeholder `results.json` (`{"seq":0,"contests":{}}`) ships with the release and is listed in `STATIC`, so the
  site never answers 404 for it and `build_site.py --check` never finds a published file missing.

### The rules (in `build_results.py`, each tested on its own)

All margins are in votes as well as percent. "Votes cast in the contest" means the sum of all choices' votes.

- **Counting:** no votes yet (zero votes never names anyone).
- **Outstanding-ballot bound:** the most votes still uncounted that could reach this contest. From each county's
  published counts of provisional and outstanding mail ballots when it posts them (Travis's preliminary
  reconciliation form), scaled by the contest's electorate; when unknown, the bound is set high enough that no leader
  is named on Nov 4. The bound falls to zero once a county reports its late ballots counted (final unofficial count).
- **Leading:** top choice ahead of the next by ≥ 1 point **and** by more votes than the outstanding bound (plus any
  missing county's bound). Vote for up to N: the same test between the Nth and the (N+1)th. Contests with under 1,000
  votes cast: counts only, verdict `none`, until official.
- **Too close to call:** a leader would be named but for the 1-point test; numbers show, no leader, no "trailing".
- **Majority contests with three or more choices** (declared write-ins count): top candidate over 52% (and over 50%
  after the bound) → leading; 48–52% → may go to a runoff; under 48% → headed to a runoff, the top two named only
  when second leads third by the same leader test, otherwise "headed to a runoff" with no names. **Two-choice
  majority contests** use the leading rule (with two candidates, only an exact tie means a runoff).
- **Measures (symmetric):** FOR ≥ 50.5% and the margin beyond the bound → passing; FOR ≤ 49.5% likewise → failing;
  otherwise too close. Official: passed only with more FOR than AGAINST; a tie fails.
- **No more candidates than seats** (one candidate, or vote for N with N candidates: c116, c154, c168, c176, c192,
  c199 and others found by the check): unopposed; elected once official.
- **Partial:** a county in the electorate whose file is missing, or present but not final, adds its registered voters
  in that electorate (from Detail XML's precinct registration; from Hays's precinct canvass PDF if it carries them;
  Task 1 finds out) to the bound for **every** verdict type: leader, majority, measure, the N cut, the runoff pair.
  When that number isn't known, no verdict.
- **Official:** won / didn't win, passed / failed, elected; ties: offices → "Tied: automatic recount" (§216.001, then
  §2.002), two-way majority → "Tied: goes to a runoff", measures → "Failed (tie)". A recount line in `official.json`
  shows "Recount requested" until Lane updates it.
- **Depends on another contest:** MUD and MMD confirmation failing → every contest of that district carries the note
  "The district wasn't confirmed (Proposition A failed)"; directors are still declared elected by law (Water Code
  §49.102(g)), so their result stands with the note. ESD annexations show one outcome line across the district's and
  the territory's votes ("Territory approved; district rejected → not annexed"), and each contest's title names its
  electorate.
- **Runoffs:** after the runoff date, "Headed to a runoff" becomes "Runoff held Dec 12; result not shown here".

### On the device

- **Reading picks:** `picks[cid]` holds `optionsOf()` names; `__skip` means skipped ("You skipped this race").
  There is no write-in pick in Ballotta, so no write-in line.
- **When it asks:** from Nov 4, 00:00 Austin time (the page's Austin clock; `BALLOTTA_TODAY` overrides it in tests),
  with a ballot showing: on load, on coming back into view, and every 10 minutes in view, the way the newer-release
  check asks for `version.json`; `cache: 'no-store'`. On phones the offline copy (network first) keeps the last one;
  a copy served from it is labeled "Saved copy; may be out of date". Laptops have no offline copy, so offline they
  show no results (said in README).
- **Untrusted input:** the page checks `results.json`'s shape before using any of it (numbers only where numbers go,
  known contests and choices only, every string escaped), and a malformed file shows nothing and never breaks the
  ballot; a test and a mutant feed it bad files.
- **Kill switch:** `"withdrawn": true` in `results.json` (from `official.json`) hides all results at once, with no
  release and no "newer Ballotta" bar. `RESULTS_LIVE` in `build_site.py` stays as the code rollback (a release).
- **Since you last looked:** a new key, `ballotta.resultsSeen` (the last `seq` shown, plus the last verdict shown per
  contest, for corrections). Clear everything already removes every `ballotta.*` key; the README's key list gains it
  (a test checks).
- **Even-handed display:** one neutral treatment for every choice, in the answer key's ballot order (write-ins by name
  after), no colors or "(I)" from any feed, never a ranked "Write-in" total. "Leading", never "winning", before
  official.
- **Content-Security-Policy:** unchanged (`connect-src 'self'` covers it).

### Publishing and the bump rule

- `results.json` is in `STATIC` (published), and `check_bumped.py` leaves it out of its diff, so a results update
  deploys without a release. `check_results.py` joins the Pages workflow's pipeline job before every deploy. Test:
  a change to `results.json` alone passes the bump check; anything else beside it still needs a bump.
- **Correcting a verdict:** add the bad file to `excluded.json` (or fix the map), rebuild, merge. `seq` goes up; the
  contest is marked `corrected`; voters who saw the old verdict are told.

### The Results workflow (`.github/workflows/results.yml`)

- **Job 1, read-only, no secrets:** fetches SOS (and Hays, Caldwell, Lee if decision 6), files the inbox into `raw/`,
  installs the pinned PDF libraries, parses, builds `results.json`, runs `check_results.py`, `build_site.py --check`
  and the pipeline tests, and uploads the result as an artifact. (Pull requests opened with the workflow token don't
  trigger Build check or Release check, so this job is their evidence.)
- **Job 2, write (`contents`, `pull-requests`), installs nothing:** commits the artifact to **one rolling branch**
  (`results/next`) and opens the pull request, or updates the open one; opens nothing when nothing changed. The change
  list goes in through a file (`--body-file`), and branch names through `env:`, never `${{ }}` inside `run:`.
- **When:** on a push to `source/results/inbox/**` (Lane's upload, made on `results/next`), and on a schedule at
  13:15 UTC (7:15 am Austin time after Nov 1), Nov 4 – Dec 10; by hand any time. Pinned actions, as in the other
  workflows. Task 1 proves the SOS fetch from an Actions runner (a fetch from this workspace worked; Actions runs from
  other addresses).

### The daily routine (Nov 4 → each canvass)

1. **By 8:30 am, Lane downloads** each Clarity county's Detail XML (Reports → Detail XML) and uploads the files into
   their county folders on the branch `results/next` (GitHub: Add file → Upload files; or attaches them in a Claude
   chat). About 5 minutes.
2. **The workflow** adds SOS (and the scripted counties), rebuilds, checks, and updates the one open pull request
   with the plain-words change list.
3. **Lane merges at 9 am**, whatever the list says (a fixed time, so timing can't look selective). Pages deploys
   `results.json`.
4. When a body canvasses (or a recount is requested), Lane adds its line to `official.json` in that day's pull
   request, with the link.

If no one merges on a given day, nothing new goes live; what's shown keeps its posted times, so it is older, never
wrong (decision 9).
