"Right rather than early" and "as much as possible, as quickly as possible" pull against each other. This plan

the numbers truly can't support one, and reserving "Won" for the canvass.

## Decisions already made (Lane, Oct 20)

1. **Next day, not election night.** Nothing changes on the night of Nov 3. The first results appear the morning of
   Nov 4.
2. **Unofficial first, clearly labeled; then official, contest by contest.** Every result carries "Unofficial" or
   "Official", the time its numbers were posted, and where they came from.
3. **Too close to call:** a margin under 1 point names no leader. **May go to a runoff:** in a contest that needs a
   majority, a top candidate between 48% and 52%.
4. **Updates are shown in the page** ("Since you last looked: …"). No push, no email.
5. **`results.json` is exempt from the VERSION bump rule** (its own sequence and its own check), and every change to the
   *wording* of a verdict goes through a pull request Lane merges.
6. **County files on Clarity are downloaded by hand** (Clarity refuses cloud servers). The Texas SOS feed is fetched
   by a script. If the counties say a script is welcome (Lane's notes, Oct 20), Task 9 automates it.

## Decisions for Lane (before Task 2)

1. **Where results show (recommended: three places).** A ribbon on each contest (both layouts), a Result column in
   At a glance, and one line at the top of the ballot ("Results: 14 of 31 official · oldest numbers posted Nov 6,
   9:10 am") plus "Since you last looked" when something changed. The crib sheet and landing page don't change.
   Results show for contests without a pick too, with no "your pick" line.
2. **A vote floor beside the 1-point rule (recommended: 20 votes).** In a tiny contest 1 point can be a single vote
   (MUD confirmation districts have a few dozen voters), and late mail, overseas, military and provisional ballots
   keep being counted until as late as Nov 16. Proposed: a leader is named when the margin is at least 1 point **and**
   at least 20 votes. **What that means in practice:** on Nov 4 essentially every state, county, city and school
   contest shows its leader (their margins run to thousands of votes); only contests closer than 1 point or 20 votes
   show "Too close to call". Every unofficial result also says late ballots are still being counted.
3. **"Won" only when official (recommended: yes).** You asked "who won". The ribbon says "Leading" (unofficial)
   until the body that canvasses that contest has canvassed it; then "Won". That's Nov 6–17 for local and county
   contests, Nov 21–Dec 7 for most state and federal ones, and **January 2027 for Governor and Lieutenant Governor**
   (the Legislature canvasses those two). "Apparent winner" is the alternative; it reads like a call, which Ballotta
   doesn't make.
4. **Numbers-only updates go live without waiting for you (recommended: yes).** Your rule is that verdict wording
   changes go through you. A daily update that only moves numbers and posted times (no verdict word changes on any
   contest) could merge itself once every check passes; anything that changes a verdict word waits for your merge.
   That keeps numbers fresh on days you're busy. Or: everything waits for you.
5. **Friend links (recommended: later).** "You both picked…" touches the share format; phase 2.
6. **One anonymous count (recommended: `results_seen`, no details, once per visit)**, through the privacy firewall and
   into the README's list. Or none.
7. **Hays by script (recommended: yes).** Hays's PDFs are on a server with no robots.txt that answers cloud requests,
   and **Hays deletes its unofficial PDFs once the official ones are up** (both 2025's and May 2026's are gone), so a
   missed day can't be recovered later. A twice-daily script fetch with a "Ballotta (ballotta.org)" User-Agent avoids
   that. Caldwell and Lee (ACC only) the same way if their files allow (Task 1).
8. **Release day (recommended: Nov 3, 10 pm).** The release that adds the results code shows "A newer Ballotta is
   available" to pages left open. By 10 pm on Election Day the last people in line at 7 pm have voted, and nothing
   visible changes until Nov 4. The alternative is a late evening Oct 29–31, the busiest crib-sheet days (the prompt
   would interrupt fewer people at 11 pm). Only the page code, `sw.js`, the placeholder `results.json` and the bump
   exclusion need the release; parsers, map and workflow keep changing until Nov 3 without one.
9. **One GitHub setting (recommended: yes).** Turn on "Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests", and
   leave "Automatically delete head branches" off (the results branch is reused). A ruleset requiring pull requests
   on `main` would change how every release is pushed, so it isn't proposed.
10. **The words** (Task 5's full table). Visitor-facing words go to you before they ship, as always.

## What "done" looks like

1. From the morning of Nov 4 (Austin time), a voter who finds their ballot sees each contest's result: unofficial
   numbers with a leader where the rules allow one, labeled with each source's posted time and the note that late
   ballots are still being counted; then **Official** once the body that canvasses that contest has canvassed it.
2. **Picks are compared on the device.** `results.json` is the same file for everyone; no pick, precinct or address is
   sent. The request for it is a plain GET of a public file from ballotta.org.
3. **Every change to a verdict word has been merged by Lane** (decision 4 lets numbers-only updates through on their
   own), from a pull request whose description lists the changes in plain words.
4. **No verdict from part of an electorate:** every contest's electorate (counties, and for annexations the district
   or the territory) is cited from its election order; a race-wide verdict missing a county is refused except under
   the partial-data rule.
5. **Even-handed:** every choice looks the same (no party colors, no incumbent marks from the feeds), in ballot order;
   declared write-in candidates appear by name like everyone else.
6. **Tested** against real past elections whose official results are known (Task 1), plus pipeline and browser tests
   and mutants. **No fixture results ever appear on ballotta.org.**

## How it's built

### The files

- `source/results/inbox/<county>/` — where Lane's downloads land, one folder per county (every Clarity download is
  named `detailxml.zip`), each holding a `.keep`. The build identifies a file's county from its contents and refuses
  a file in the wrong folder.
- `source/results/raw/<source>/<posted-time>-<hash8>/…` — every file, exactly as fetched or downloaded, named by **the
  time posted inside the file** (Detail XML's timestamp; the PDF's Run Date and Run Time; SOS's `LastUpdatedTime`,
  whose time zone Task 1 settles) plus the first 8 characters of its hash (so a changed file with an unchanged
  timestamp can't collide). Committed only when the hash is new; SOS `countyInfo` isn't fetched. Never edited.
- `source/results/excluded.json` — raw files to ignore, each with a reason, a date and the contests it affects: how a
  bad download is corrected without editing history, and how a correction is recognized.
- `source/results_map.json` — one entry per contest in the answer key (214; the two "declared elected unopposed"
  groups aren't contests and need none):
  - `source`: `sos` or `counties`; `canvasser`: the body whose canvass makes it official (`state`, `legislature`,
    `travis-county`, `city-of-austin`, `austin-isd`, `tcad`, `eastwood-mud`, …)
  - `electorate`: counties (or district / territory), each with a citation (election order URL and page)
  - `keys`: each source's name or ID for the contest
  - `choices`: each choice → each source's spelling, **keyed by the names the page stores picks under** (`optionsOf()`,
    template L1236: candidate names without the party suffix; FOR / AGAINST), plus every **declared write-in
    candidate**, listed by name and marked write-in
  - `seats` (the page's `seats()`), `majority` (sourced: city charters, ISD and college rules, TCAD; state, federal and
    county general-election offices are plurality, §2.001)
  - `depends_on`: MUD and MMD confirmation propositions; ESD annexations voted separately by district and territory
- `source/results/official.json` — written by hand: one line per canvassing body, `{canvasser, canvassed: date,
  link}`; optional `recount: {requested: date, link}`; and `withdrawn: true` (the kill switch). A contest is Official
  only when **its own canvasser's** line is here and its official numbers are loaded. The check refuses a canvass date
  outside the legal window (local Nov 6–17; state Nov 21–Dec 7; legislature after Jan 12, 2027).
- `source/build_results.py` — reads all of the above **plus the `results.json` published before it** (the ledger:
  see `seq`), and writes **`results.json`** deterministically (no clock reads), plus the plain-words change list for
  the pull request, and whether any verdict word changed (decision 4).
- `source/check_results.py` — stdlib only, so it can run in the deploy job as well as the pipeline job. Refuses:
  an answer-key contest missing from the map; **in a contest Ballotta shows**, a source choice that maps to nothing
  (contests not on Travis ballots are ignored by the source's own county or district fields); a counties contest with
  an uncited county; Official through anyone but the contest's canvasser, or outside its window; a source's posted time
  going backwards; a `seq` that isn't the previous one plus one; numbers that don't add up (choices vs. totals; county
  sums vs. an entity's canvass); and, in the pipeline job, a committed `results.json` that differs from a fresh build
  against the previous one (`build_site.py --check` does the same for the page).

### `results.json`

```
{ "seq": 7, "election": "2026-11-03", "withdrawn": false,
  "contests": { "c071": {
      "state": "counting" | "unofficial" | "partial" | "official" | "recount",
      "verdict": "none" | "leading" | "too_close" | "may_runoff" | "runoff" | "won" | "elected" | "passing" | "failing"
                 | "passed" | "failed" | "failed_tie" | "unopposed" | "tied" | "tied_legislature" | "tied_runoff"
                 | "tied_second",
      "leaders": ["FOR"], "runoff": [],
      "choices": [["FOR", 123456, 61.2], ["AGAINST", 78345, 38.8]],   // ballot order; declared write-ins by name after
      "missing": ["hays"], "travis": [["FOR", 100345, 62.0], ["AGAINST", 61500, 38.0]],
      "posted": { "travis": "2026-11-04T08:41", "williamson": "2026-11-04T07:55", "hays": null },
      "changed": 6, "corrected": false, "note": "district_not_confirmed" | null } } }
```

- **`seq`** is the previous `results.json`'s `seq` plus one whenever the contests change (the previous file is an
  input; the placeholder starts at 0). **`changed`** is the `seq` at which this contest's state or verdict last
  changed (carried from the previous file, or set now). "Since you last looked" counts contests whose `changed` is above
  the `seq` this device last showed.
- **`corrected`** is set only when `excluded.json` (or a map fix) changes a verdict already published; ordinary
  movement as late ballots come in (Leading → Too close) is never called a correction.
- A **placeholder** `results.json` (`{"seq":0,"election":"2026-11-03","withdrawn":false,"contests":{}}`) ships in
  `STATIC` with the release, so the site never answers 404 and `build_site.py --check` never finds it missing.

### The rules (in `build_results.py`, each tested on its own)

"Votes cast in the contest" = the sum of all choices' votes. A margin is between two choices' votes.

- **Counting:** no votes yet. Zero votes never names anyone.
- **Leading:** top choice ahead of the next by ≥ 1 point **and** ≥ 20 votes (decision 2). Vote for up to N: the same
  test between the Nth and the (N+1)th, naming the top N.
- **Too close to call:** a leader would be named but for that test. Numbers show; no leader; no "trailing".
- **Majority contests with three or more choices** (declared write-ins count): top candidate over 52% → leading;
  48–52% → may go to a runoff; under 48% → headed to a runoff, the top two named only when second beats third by the
  leading test, otherwise "headed to a runoff" without names. **Two-choice majority contests** use the leading rule
  (with two candidates only an exact tie means a runoff).
- **Measures (symmetric):** FOR ≥ 50.5% and FOR − AGAINST ≥ 20 votes → passing; FOR ≤ 49.5% likewise → failing;
  otherwise too close. Official: passed only with more FOR than AGAINST; an exact tie fails.
- **Unopposed:** no more named candidates than seats **and no declared write-in** (one candidate; or c116, c154, c168,
  c176, c192, c199, vote for N with N candidates): unopposed; elected once official. A contest with a declared
  write-in (c053 County Clerk has one) follows the other rules.
- **Partial:** a county in the electorate whose file is **missing** (not yet downloaded, or not posted) adds that
  county's registered voters in that electorate (from Detail XML's precinct registration; from Hays's precinct canvass
  PDF, which lists registered voters per precinct) to the margin every verdict must clear: the leader, the majority
  zone, a measure, the N cut, the runoff pair. When that number isn't known, no verdict until the file arrives.
- **Official:** won / didn't win, elected, passed / failed. **Ties:** offices → "Tied: state law decides (drawing
  lots, a recount or a new election)" (§2.002); Governor, Lt. Governor, Attorney General, Comptroller and Land
  Commissioner (c006–c010) → "Tied: the Legislature decides" (§2.002(h); Const. art. IV §3); a two-way majority contest
  → "Tied: goes to a runoff" (§2.023(a)); a tie for second in a majority contest → "Tie for the runoff spot: a recount
  decides" (§2.023(c)); measures → "Failed (tie)". A recount line in `official.json` shows "Recount requested".
- **Depends on another contest:** a MUD or MMD confirmation that fails puts the note "The district wasn't confirmed
  (Proposition A failed)" on every contest of that district; directors are still declared elected by the temporary
  board (Water Code §49.102(g) directs it to declare them, and says nothing of a defeated district), so their result
  stands with the note. ESD annexations get one outcome line across the district's and the territory's votes
  ("Territory approved; district rejected → not annexed"), and each contest's title names its electorate.
- **Runoffs:** after the runoff date, "Headed to a runoff" becomes "Runoff held Dec 12; result not shown here".

### On the device

- **Reading picks:** `picks[cid]` holds `optionsOf()` names (read at template L1320); `__skip` (L1355) means skipped
  ("You skipped this race"). Ballotta has no write-in pick, so there's no write-in line.
- **When it asks:** from Nov 4, 00:00 Austin time (the page's Austin clock), with a ballot showing: on load, on coming
  back into view, and every 10 minutes in view, like the newer-release check's `version.json` (L2945); `cache:
  'no-store'`.
- **Testing the date gate:** a new override, `?today=YYYY-MM-DD`, honored **only** when the page's host is `localhost`
  or `127.0.0.1` (a test proves ballotta.org ignores it). Tests also keep using `BALLOTTA_TODAY` (Playwright's clock).
- **The saved copy:** on phones the offline copy (network first, `sw.js`) keeps the last `results.json`. `sw.js` gains
  one change: a kept `results.json` it serves carries a header (`X-Ballotta-Copy: saved`), so the page can say "Saved
  copy; may be out of date". Laptops have no offline copy, so offline they show no results (said in the README).
- **Untrusted input:** the page checks `results.json`'s shape before using any of it (numbers only where numbers go,
  known contests and choices only, every string escaped); a malformed file shows nothing and never breaks the ballot.
- **Kill switch:** `withdrawn: true` (set in `official.json`, rebuilt, merged) hides all results at once, with no
  release and no "newer Ballotta" bar. `RESULTS_LIVE` in `build_site.py` stays as the code rollback (a release).
- **Since you last looked:** a new key, `ballotta.resultsSeen` (the last `seq` shown, plus the last verdict shown per
  contest, so a correction can be named). Clear everything already removes every `ballotta.*` key; the README's key
  list gains it (a test checks).
- **Even-handed display:** one neutral treatment for every choice, in ballot order with declared write-ins by name
  after; no colors or "(I)" from any feed; never a ranked "Write-in" total. "Leading", never "winning", before official.
- **Content-Security-Policy:** unchanged (`connect-src 'self'` covers it).

### Publishing and the bump rule

- `results.json` is in `STATIC`, and `check_bumped.py` leaves it out of its diff, so a results update deploys without
  a release. Both land **in the release** (Task 7), since adding a published file is itself a change the bump check
  would otherwise refuse. Test: a change to `results.json` alone passes the bump check; anything else beside it still
  needs a bump.
- `check_results.py` runs in the Pages workflow's pipeline job (with the fresh-build comparison) **and in its deploy
  job** (stdlib only, like `check_bumped.py`), so even a deploy run with "skip the pipeline tests" checks it.
- **Correcting a verdict:** add the bad file to `excluded.json` with the contests it affects, push to the results
  branch (which rebuilds), merge. `seq` goes up; those contests are marked `corrected`; voters who saw the old verdict
  are told "Corrected since you last looked".

### The Results workflow (`.github/workflows/results.yml`)

- **Triggers:** a push to the branch `results/next` touching `source/results/**` or `source/results_map.json`
  (Lane's uploads, `official.json`, `excluded.json`, map fixes: every human input rebuilds); a schedule at 13:15 and
  23:15 UTC (7:15 am and 5:15 pm Austin time after Nov 1), Nov 4 – Dec 10; by hand any time. Its own commits, made
  with the workflow token, don't trigger it again.
- **Job 1, read-only, no secrets:** fetches SOS (and Hays, Caldwell, Lee if decision 7), files the inbox into `raw/`,
  installs the pinned PDF libraries, builds `results.json` against the one on `main`, runs `check_results.py`,
  `build_site.py --check` and the pipeline tests, and uploads the result as an artifact. (Pull requests opened with the
  workflow token don't trigger Build check or Release check, so this job is their evidence.)
- **Job 2, write (`contents`, `pull-requests`), installs nothing:** commits the artifact to `results/next` and opens or
  updates the one pull request; opens nothing when nothing changed. If no verdict word changed and decision 4 is yes,
  it merges the pull request itself. The change list goes in through `--body-file`; branch names through `env:`,
  never `${{ }}` inside `run:`.
- **After a merge** (merge commits, never squash, so the branch never diverges): Job 2, on the push to `main` that
  changes `results.json`, resets `results/next` to `main`, so Lane's next upload starts clean.
- Pinned actions, as in the other workflows. Task 1 proves the SOS fetch from an Actions runner.

### The daily routine (Nov 4 → each canvass)

1. **By 8:30 am, Lane downloads** each Clarity county's Detail XML (Travis, Bastrop, and Williamson if it's on
   Clarity; Williamson's PDF if not; Hays's PDF too if decision 7 is no) and uploads them into their county folders on
   `results/next` (GitHub: Add file → Upload files; or attaches them in a Claude chat). About 5–10 minutes.
2. **The workflow** adds SOS (and the scripted counties), rebuilds, checks, and updates the one open pull request
   with the plain-words change list.
3. **Lane merges by 9 am** when the list shows verdict changes, every day at the same time so timing can't look
   selective; he holds a merge only for an error in the data, and says why in the pull request. Numbers-only updates
   merge themselves (decision 4).
4. When a body canvasses (or a recount is requested), Lane adds its line to `official.json` on `results/next`, with
   the link; the push rebuilds.

If nothing is merged on a given day, what's shown stays as it was, with its posted times, so it is older and says so.

## Task 3: parsers and rules

`build_results.py`: SOS feed (`election/{id}` is an object whose sections are each base64 JSON, except `Version`, a
plain string; `electionConstants` and `countyInfo` wrap theirs in `{"upload": …}`), SOS Official Canvass Report PDF,
Clarity Detail XML, Hays PDF, Williamson PDF (in case Nov 3 isn't on Clarity), Caldwell and Lee (format from Task 1).
