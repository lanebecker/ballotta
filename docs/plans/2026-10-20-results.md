# Ballotta: results on your ballot — build plan

*Written Oct 20, 2026 for Lane. Status: **draft for Lane's review.** Unlike the street map's plan, nothing here has
been dry-run on a copy of the repo yet: Task 1 (the rehearsals on real, already-official elections) is that dry run,
and its findings will be folded back into Tasks 2–7 before any page code is written. Sources and API details:
the Ballotta project doc `claude/ballotta-results-sources.md` (Oct 20), summarized under "Where the numbers come from".*

## The request (verbatim)

> "is there an automated way to pull information about who won each election in Travis County on November 4 and
> later? If so, I'd like to explore how we might show people a version of their ballot that tells who won, and whether
> or not it matched their pick, when they come back after the election has ended."

On timing and sources (Oct 20):

> "I don't think this is a same-night feature; it's a next-day feature."

> "I do want to give people as much info as possible as quickly as possible -- can we show them results that are
> unofficial and make that clear as we get the detail — again, the next day is fine, it doesn't need to be immediate,
> but waiting until December for any results is too long. Need to give them reasonably timed updates, including when
> something has been made official."

On getting the county files:

> "let's assume your fallback plan of me downloading and you parsing is the likely outcome and build with that
> assumption in mind."

## Decisions already made (Lane, Oct 20)

1. **Next day, not election night.** Nothing changes on the night of Nov 3. The first results appear the morning of
   Nov 4.
2. **Unofficial first, clearly labeled; then official, contest by contest.** Every result carries "Unofficial" or
   "Official", the time its numbers were posted, and where they came from.
3. **Too close to call:** a margin under 1 point names no leader. **May go to a runoff:** in a contest that needs a
   majority, a top candidate between 48% and 52%.
4. **Updates are shown in the page** ("Since you last looked: 12 results became official."). No push, no email.
5. **`results.json` is exempt from the VERSION bump rule** (its own stamp and its own check), but every change to the
   *wording* of a verdict goes through a pull request Lane merges.
6. **County files are downloaded by hand** (Travis, Williamson, Bastrop on Clarity; Hays PDFs). Only the Texas SOS
   feed is fetched by a script. If the counties say a script is welcome (Lane's notes, Oct 20), Task 9 automates it.

## Decisions for Lane (before Task 2)

1. **Where results show (recommended: three places).** (a) A ribbon on each contest on the ballot paper (both
   layouts), under the contest title; (b) a Result column in At a glance; (c) one line at the top of the ballot:
   "Results: 14 of 31 official · updated Nov 6, 9:10 am" plus the "Since you last looked" line when something
   changed. The crib sheet stays as it is (it's for the polls). The landing page doesn't change.
2. **Contests you didn't pick (recommended: show them).** The result shows with no "your pick" line. A ballot with
   no picks at all still gets results.
3. **Friend links (recommended: later).** "You both picked the winner in 7 of 12" is a good share hook, but it touches
   the share format; phase 2, after Nov 4 is working.
4. **One anonymous count (recommended: `results_seen`, no details, once per visit).** Tells us whether anyone comes
   back after the election. It goes through the privacy firewall like every event and into the README's list. Or none.
5. **The words** (Task 5 lists every string). Visitor-facing words go to Lane before they ship, as always.
6. **When to release the results code (recommended: a quiet evening, Oct 29–31).** It is a release (1.4.0), so pages
   left open show "A newer Ballotta is available" once, during the last days of early voting. With no `results.json`
   published, nothing visible changes until Nov 4.

## What "done" looks like

1. From the morning of Nov 4 (Austin time), a voter who opens Ballotta and finds their ballot sees each contest's
   result: unofficial numbers with a leader (or "too close to call", "may go to a runoff", "partial"), then
   **Official** once that contest's canvass is in, with the time posted and the source.
2. **Their picks are compared on the device.** `results.json` is the same file for everyone; no pick, precinct or
   address is ever sent. The request for it is a plain GET of a public file from ballotta.org, like the page itself.
3. **Every winner Ballotta names has been seen by Lane:** each change to a verdict arrives as a pull request whose
   description lists, in words, what changes ("City of Austin Proposition A: — → Unofficial · passing, 61.2%").
4. **No verdict from Travis numbers alone** for a contest that crosses county lines: the mapping table names every
   contest's counties (sourced) and the check refuses a race-wide verdict missing one, except under the partial-data
   rule.
5. **Tested** against real past elections whose official results are known (Task 1), plus browser tests and mutants
   as usual.
6. **Updates on a schedule voters can count on:** SOS numbers every morning; county numbers on the mornings Lane
   uploads them (Nov 4 through each canvass); "Official" as each canvass posts (county Nov 6–17; state Nov 21–Dec 7).

## How it's built

### The files

- `source/results/raw/<source>/<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>/…` — every download, committed exactly as fetched (SOS JSON, Clarity
  zip or JSON, Hays PDF), named by the time posted. Never edited; a bad download is replaced by a new one.
- `source/results_map.json` — one entry per contest on the answer key (214), written once and reviewed by Lane:
  - `authority`: `sos`, `travis`, `sum`, or `none` (declared elected unopposed, not on the ballot)
  - `counties`: for `sum`, each county with a citation (election order URL and page)
  - `keys`: each source's name or ID for the contest (SOS race ID; Clarity contest name per county; Hays PDF heading)
  - `choices`: each answer-key choice name → each source's spelling (Write-in included)
  - `seats` (vote for up to N, read from the title as the page already does), `majority` (true where a runoff
    applies, sourced: city charters, ISD board rules; state, federal and county general-election races are plurality)
- `source/results/official.json` — written by hand, one line per canvass: `{entity or "state", canvassed: date, link}`.
  A contest is Official only when its authority's line is here **and** the numbers come from a file posted after
  that date. This is where Lane's judgment sits, on purpose.
- `source/build_results.py` — reads the raw files, the map and `official.json`; writes **`results.json`**
  deterministically (same inputs, same bytes) and a plain-words summary of what changed since the last
  `results.json` (for the pull request).
- `source/check_results.py` — refuses: a contest on the answer key missing from the map; a source contest or choice
  that maps to nothing; a `sum` contest with a county the map doesn't cite; a stamp older than the last one published;
  "Official" without an `official.json` line; any number that doesn't add up (choices vs. total cast, county sums vs.
  race-wide totals where both exist).

### `results.json`

```
{ "stamp": "2026-11-04T09:10-06:00", "election": "2026-11-03",
  "contests": { "c071": {
      "state": "unofficial" | "partial" | "official" | "counting",
      "verdict": "leading" | "too_close" | "may_runoff" | "runoff" | "won" | "passing" | "failing" | "unopposed" | "tied",
      "leaders": ["FOR"], "runoff": [],
      "choices": { "FOR": [123456, 61.2], "AGAINST": [78345, 38.8] },
      "missing": ["Hays"], "travis": { "FOR": [100345, 62.0], "AGAINST": [61500, 38.0] },
      "posted": "2026-11-04T08:41-06:00", "sources": ["travis", "williamson", "hays"] } } }
```

`travis` is "How Travis County voted" for contests decided beyond the county (state races from SOS's county
breakdown; multi-county locals from Travis's own file). Precinct-level numbers are in the downloads but not in v1.

### The rules (in `build_results.py`, tested on their own)

- **Leading:** top choice ahead of the next by at least 1 point of the votes cast in the contest. For "vote for up to
  N": the Nth and the (N+1)th, 1 point apart.
- **Too close to call:** under 1 point. No leader named; the numbers still show.
- **Majority contests:** top candidate over 52% → leading; 48–52% → may go to a runoff; under 48% with nobody over
  50% → headed to a runoff, top two named (once a 1-point gap separates second from third).
- **Measures:** FOR over 50% by at least 0.5 (1 point between FOR and AGAINST) → passing; under → failing; otherwise
  too close.
- **Partial:** a `sum` contest with a county's file missing shows the counted numbers, "Partial — Hays County not yet
  included", and names a leader only if the margin is larger than every vote the missing slice could add (its
  registered voters for that entity, from the county's last report; if unknown, no leader).
- **Official:** won / lost, passed / failed, runoff (who), tied (the entity decides) — only via `official.json`.
- **Unopposed:** "Unopposed"; "Elected" once official.

### On the device

- From Nov 4, 00:00 Austin time (the page already keeps an Austin clock), with a ballot showing, the page asks
  `results.json` with `cache: 'no-store'`. The offline copy (`sw.js`, network first) keeps the last one, so offline
  shows the last results with their stamp. A 404 (before the first upload) shows nothing.
- Picks are compared on the device: `picks[cid]` holds choice names, which are `results.json`'s keys.
  "Your pick is leading / trailing", "1 of your 2 picks is winning", "You wrote in a name; write-ins aren't
  counted by name here".
- **Since you last looked:** a new key, `ballotta.resultsSeen` (the stamp last shown). The README's key list and
  **Clear everything on this device** take it, and a test checks both.
- **Content-Security-Policy:** unchanged (`connect-src 'self'` already covers it).

### Publishing and the bump rule

- `results.json` is published like the page (`site_files()`), but `check_bumped.py` leaves it out of its diff, so a
  results update deploys without a release. `check_results.py` runs in the Pages workflow's pipeline job before every
  deploy instead. A test: a change to `results.json` alone passes the bump check; a change to anything else beside
  it still needs a bump.
- **A wrong verdict is fixed in minutes:** revert or correct the raw file or `official.json`, rebuild, merge. No
  release, no "newer Ballotta" bar.

### The daily routine (Nov 4 → canvass)

1. **Each morning, Lane downloads** from each county's results site (Reports → Detail XML, or the summary file:
   Task 1 settles which button is easiest) and Hays's latest PDF, and uploads them on GitHub (Add file → Upload
   files into `source/results/inbox/`, on a new branch `results/<date>`), or attaches them in a Claude chat. About
   5 minutes.
2. **The Results workflow** (on a push to `results/**`, and every morning at 7:15 for SOS alone): fetches SOS, files
   the inbox into `raw/`, builds, checks, and opens a pull request whose description is the plain-words change list.
3. **Lane reads the list and merges.** Pages deploys `results.json`.
4. When a canvass posts, Lane adds its line to `official.json` in the same pull request as that day's downloads.

If Lane can't do a morning, SOS-only updates still arrive as pull requests, and the county numbers wait a day,
labeled with the time they were posted, so nothing shown is ever wrong, only older.

## Task 1: the rehearsals (the dry run) — real elections, already official

Each rehearsal downloads real files, runs a first version of the parsers and rules, and compares the result with the
official canvass. What it teaches goes back into this plan; trimmed copies of the files become test fixtures in
`tests/fixtures/results/`.

1. **City of Austin Prop Q, Nov 4, 2025 (three counties).** Travis Clarity (election 124687) + Williamson (Nov 2025
   was PDF) + Hays (`Cumulative Results 20251104 Official.pdf`), summed, against the City of Austin's official
   canvass of Prop Q. Expect an exact match. This tests summing, the Hays PDF parser and the partial rule (drop Hays,
   check the bound).
2. **Hays Nov 2025, unofficial → official.** Hays posted both `Cumulative Results Unofficial.pdf` and the official
   one: a real before-and-after, to test the change list and the Unofficial → Official step.
3. **The 17 constitutional amendments, Nov 4, 2025.** SOS (51031) vs. Travis Clarity: SOS's Travis numbers stopped
   on Nov 6 (Prop 1: 222,224 vs. Travis's official 222,442). A test that SOS's freeze can never make anything
   Official.
4. **The May 26, 2026 primary runoff.** SOS (58314, official, county breakdown) vs. Travis Clarity (126357): race-wide
   from SOS, the Travis slice equal in both.
5. **May 2, 2026 locals, by hand.** Lane downloads Travis's files (126203) the way he will on Nov 4, timed: proves the
   button, the upload path and the 5-minute estimate.
6. **Nov 3, 2026, empty.** SOS 53815 is live with zero votes: map all 41 SOS races now. When Travis's and Williamson's
   Nov 3 Clarity sites appear (usually before Election Day, with zeros), Lane downloads them once and Task 2 maps
   every county contest before any votes exist.

## Risks

- **Williamson's Nov 3 results aren't on Clarity** (it only moved there in May): then its numbers come from PDFs,
  like Hays's. Task 1 builds the PDF path anyway.
- **A county site changes shape on Nov 4:** the check refuses; those contests stay at their last numbers with their
  stamp until fixed.
- **The SOS API changes** (it's an internal API, version `tx-oct14-prod-global-v3-20261017.1` on Oct 20): the
  workflow fails loudly; Lane can download the SOS race pages by hand too.
- **Names don't match** (Clarity's contest titles vs. the bedsheet's): the map is explicit, and unmatched names stop
  the build.
- **Lane is busy some morning:** results are a day older, still labeled with their time; never wrong.
