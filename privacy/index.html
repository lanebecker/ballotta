How Ballotta handles your privacy

Updated October 31, 2026

Ballotta is a free, independent tool for Travis County voters. It’s built so that it never receives your address, your picks or your reasons. This page says exactly where everything goes.

Your address

When you look up your ballot, your browser sends your address straight to Travis County’s public map server to find your voting districts. If the county can’t match it, your browser asks the U.S. Census Bureau’s address lookup instead. Ballotta never receives your address.

Your browser keeps your precinct, the contests on your ballot and a map point for your address, so your next visit skips the lookup and still shows the vote centers closest to you. The address itself isn’t kept.

Your picks and reasons

Your picks, your reasons and your share name are saved in this browser, on this device, along with a few settings, like where you left off. They aren’t sent anywhere. To remove them, choose Clear everything on this device in Feedback & Info.

Keep my picks and Share my picks make a link that carries your picks (and your reasons, if you include them) inside the link itself, after the “#”. Browsers never send that part of a link to any server, Ballotta’s included. Anyone who has the link can see what’s in it, so send a Keep my picks link only to yourself.

On a phone, Ballotta keeps a copy of the site on your device, so it opens without a connection.

Election results

From Nov 4, your browser downloads the same results file as everyone else and matches it to your picks on your device. Nothing about your ballot is sent.

What we count

Ballotta uses Umami Cloud to count page views, how quickly pages load, and a fixed list of anonymous actions, like “a ballot was found”, “a share link was opened” or “the mailing list was opened”. It sets no cookies.

  • Counts never include your address, precinct, districts, which race, candidate names, your picks, your reasons or your name.
  • Each count says whether it came from the phone or the laptop layout (and, on a phone, whether from the Home Screen app). With it, Umami receives your screen size, your browser’s language and the site you came from.
  • A shared link carries a random code, so we can count how often a link is opened without knowing what’s in it.
  • Umami says it uses your IP address only to estimate your country, and doesn’t store it.
  • If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, Ballotta counts nothing at all, not even page views.

The full list of counts, and the only details each may carry, is in Ballotta’s README. This page counts nothing.

Hosting

GitHub Pages serves ballotta.org. Like most web hosts, GitHub logs the IP address of every visit, for security. Ballotta doesn’t see those logs. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

The mailing list

If you sign up to be emailed, you give your email address to Buttondown, which sends Ballotta’s emails, on Buttondown’s own page. It’s never connected to your address or your picks. We use it only to tell you when election results are up and when your next ballot is ready, and you can unsubscribe from any email. Ballotta’s emails don’t track opens or clicks. See Buttondown’s privacy policy.

Feedback

Send feedback opens your own email app with a message to Ballotta. It fills in your precinct and how the lookup went, never your address, and you can change or delete anything before you send it.

Links to other sites

Ballotta links to the League of Women Voters guide, campaign sites, official sources, Google Maps directions and the county’s wait-times map. Those sites have their own privacy policies. A directions link gives Google the vote center’s address, never yours.

What we don’t do

No accounts, no ads, no paid placement, and no selling or sharing data.
